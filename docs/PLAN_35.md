# Plan 35 record: grounded offers and reforms

## Live A/B (2026-09-25, one sample per arm, 2 of 6 scenarios)

Fixtures were generated by the pre-change binary (`4dd06c1`), so both arms start from identical
saves without `intent_version`. Qwen3.5-4B Q4_K_M
(`00fe7986…ef11a4`), llama.cpp `b11011-aa39d7a3e`, 32K context, 1 slot, M1 Pro. The arms ran in the
order before/peace, plan35/peace, plan35/access, before/access through
`tools/adjudication_compare.py --baseline-only`, one manifest per arm and scenario.

| Scenario | Before: outcome | Before: median s/month | Plan 35: outcome | Plan 35: median s/month |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| peace | passed | 121.9 (100.2–143.5) | passed | 99.0 (97.5–100.4) |
| access_ceasefire | passed | 116.0 (104.7–127.3) | **missed** | 146.0 (125.3–166.5) |

No retries or truncations in any arm. Output tokens: peace 5,227 before vs 4,168 after;
access 5,113 before vs 6,165 after.

Access, month by month:

- Before: month 1 produced prose only. Month 2 recorded both offers (a trade agreement granting
  PT access, and a ceasefire). In plan 34 the baseline recorded the access offer in none of its
  screened runs, so this pass is the model's run-to-run variation.
- Plan 35: month 1 recorded the ceasefire twice, one copy with a duplicated `end_conflict` clause.
  The `maxItems: 2` on offer outcomes invited this; identical-offer dedupe does not catch
  differing terms.
- Plan 35: month 2 recorded the access offer as a structured proposal (`defence_pact`), but with
  `grant_access.to = ES`, the reverse of the order. The narrowed recipient enum lists `ES` before
  `PT`, and the 4B takes the first enum value.

## Re-run after two fixes (same day, plan 35 access only)

The fixes: offer outcomes allow one operation (`maxItems: 1`), and the offering nation comes first in
the party and recipient enums. Result: **missed** again. Median 133.0 s/month (117.7–148.3);
no retries or truncations; 6,080 output tokens.

- Month 1 recorded one ceasefire, but its only clause ceded a Portuguese region to Spain. It
  cannot take effect without the player's signature.
- Month 2 recorded the access order as a `trade_agreement` with no clauses.

Across both samples the narrowing reliably makes the model write *a* treaty, but not the
right clauses, and it costs 15–26% on the access scenario.

## Decision

- **Reforms are adopted by default.** The peace gate is met on this sample: the project was
  correct in both months, 19% faster.
- **Offer narrowing is not adopted.** It and the `clauses` key are the opt-in `offers`
  experiment. The base prompt is back to its original wording.
- **The intent version 1 compiler rules stay on**: policy-labelled projects, and access-only
  ceasefires or peace treaties relabelled `other`.
- **Player treaty offers remain open.** Grammar narrowing gets the 4B to write a treaty, but not
  its clauses. A structural answer probably has to avoid model-drafted clauses altogether.

The measured peace arm also carried the `clauses` key and prompt wording, since offer narrowing was
not yet opt-in. The adopted default differs only in treaty wording, which the peace orders do not
use; the default configuration itself was not re-run.

One sample per arm cannot support tail or general quality claims. Windows is untested.
